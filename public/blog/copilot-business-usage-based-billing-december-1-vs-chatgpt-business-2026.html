Short answer: starting December 1, 2026, new Microsoft 365 Copilot Business licenses bought through Microsoft's partner (CSP) channel come with usage-based billing switched on by default. The seat price stays the same, but certain advanced features, including Copilot Cowork, now draw on pay-as-you-go Copilot Credits. The default spending limit is 4,000 credits per user per month, which Microsoft partners Sherweb and Universal.cloud put at about $40 per user per month. For a five-person team, that is up to $200 a month in possible extra charges on top of the seat fees unless an admin lowers the limit. ChatGPT Business works differently. You pay a fixed per-seat price, and OpenAI's help article describes extra workspace credits as something you purchase for usage beyond the included limits.

This is not a reason to panic if you already use Copilot. Grey Matter, a Microsoft partner, says the default is a ceiling, not a bill: you are charged only for credits actually used. But it changes how you should compare the two products. The question is no longer only "$18 or $21 a seat versus $20 or $25 a seat." It is also "who in my business can turn on metered features, and who is watching the meter?" Below: what changes, what it could cost a small team, the settings to check before December 1, and when each product is the better fit.

What Microsoft Is Changing on December 1

Microsoft announced the change to its partners in Partner Center and later moved the start date from November 2 to December 1, 2026. Microsoft's October 2026 Partner Center announcements (last updated October 29) and partner write-ups from Sherweb (October 16), Grey Matter (October 14) and Universal.cloud agree on the main points:

  • What: usage-based billing is on by default for new Microsoft 365 Copilot Business purchases through CSP, including standalone licenses and bundles. Sherweb adds that it is also switched on when you add Copilot Business seats to an existing subscription, upgrade to a Copilot Business SKU, or move to a new partner.
  • How it is billed: Microsoft sets up an Azure subscription with a spending policy. In Sherweb's words, the default is "capped at 4,000 Copilot Credits, approximately $40 USD, per user per month. Billing is pay-as-you-go, so customers are charged for their actual usage within that policy." Universal.cloud lists pay-as-you-go at $0.01 per credit, billed monthly in arrears.
  • Where: Microsoft says the rollout initially excludes Australia, Belgium, Brazil, France, Germany, India, Italy, Korea, the Netherlands, Poland and Spain.
  • Base license: Microsoft's announcement says: "Beginning December 1, 2026, new Microsoft 365 Copilot Business purchases move to new offers that don't require a qualifying base subscription." Existing subscriptions are unchanged.

The partners disagree on some details, which is itself a reason to check with whoever sells you Microsoft 365. Grey Matter says that if you already have Copilot Business and buy one more eligible license on or after December 1, the spending policy "will apply to both your new and existing Copilot Business licences." It also says Microsoft has made an exception for tenants that already hold a Microsoft 365 Copilot (enterprise) or ME7 license. Universal.cloud says Microsoft "does not mention an automatic change for existing Copilot Business licenses." Sherweb says the change covers every Business plan bundled with Copilot, while the other two describe it as Copilot Business only.

What Uses Credits, and What Doesn't

The everyday features most small teams bought Copilot for are not metered. Grey Matter says "summaries, drafting and basic analysis as well as auto model selection for included models will not be an additional cost." The metered features are the newer, longer-running ones. Microsoft's usage-based billing overview (updated October 30) lists the services that consume Copilot Credits:

  • Copilot Cowork (Copilot license required)
  • Advanced work in SharePoint and OneDrive (Copilot license required)
  • Work IQ API
  • Copilot Managed Runtime, including apps hosted on it
  • Teams Phone Agent
  • Code (Copilot license required)

The same page says the "Auto-apply new services" setting is enabled by default for spending policies. In plain terms, when Microsoft adds a new metered service later, it can start drawing on your spending policy unless an admin has turned that setting off. Universal.cloud also says Cowork is available to all users by default in the agent settings. We could not confirm that on Microsoft's own pages, so treat it as something to check, not a fact.

The practical line: after December 1, a Copilot Business seat has a fixed part and a metered part. The fixed part covers what most people use day to day. The metered part covers agent-style work, and its default ceiling is about $40 per user per month. Whether that is useful flexibility or a surprise on the invoice depends entirely on whether someone in your business sets the limits.

Already on Copilot? Five Settings to Check Before December 1

If you are staying with Microsoft, or adding seats after December 1, an hour with your admin center now prevents a surprise later. Microsoft's usage-based billing overview says admins manage this in the Microsoft 365 admin center under Copilot > Cost management.

  1. Find out whether the change applies to you. Ask your Microsoft provider whether your licenses come through CSP, whether you are in a covered country, and whether adding seats will apply the policy to your whole tenant, as Grey Matter describes.
  2. Set the spending limit you actually want. Microsoft says admins can configure policy-level and user-level spending limits. Grey Matter says that if you do not want these features, "you can set the spending limit to zero to disable usage-based billing."
  3. Turn off "Auto-apply new services" if you want new metered services to need approval first. Microsoft says the setting is on by default.
  4. Decide who gets Cowork. Sherweb says admins can turn off usage-based experiences such as Cowork in the admin center so they are not visible to users. Give it to the one or two people who will use it well, and nobody else.
  5. Set budget alerts. Microsoft's pay-as-you-go documentation says admins can add a budget limit and get email notifications at percentage milestones. Send them to whoever pays the bills, not only to IT.

When Copilot Still Makes Sense, and When ChatGPT Business Does

We sell ChatGPT Business, so weigh this accordingly. Copilot Business is a strong choice if your team lives in Outlook, Teams and SharePoint, you already pay for Microsoft 365, and someone owns the admin center. In that case, the December change is a setting to manage, not a reason to switch. The metered agents can also be useful. You just want to choose to pay for them.

ChatGPT Business tends to be the better fit when your tools are mixed (Google Workspace, QuickBooks, a CRM, Slack), when nobody on the team wants to manage Azure spending policies, or when you want one predictable per-seat bill. OpenAI says it does not train on ChatGPT Business data by default. For small professional practices, the fixed bill matters too. Some businesses will keep both. A common setup is to keep Microsoft 365 for email and documents and use ChatGPT Business as the AI workspace. If you do that, you probably do not need the Copilot add-on at all, and the December change does not affect you.

Frequently Asked Questions

What changes for Microsoft 365 Copilot Business on December 1, 2026? ▼

Microsoft is turning on usage-based billing by default for new Copilot Business licenses bought through its CSP partner channel. Advanced features such as Copilot Cowork, Work IQ API and advanced SharePoint and OneDrive work draw on pay-as-you-go Copilot Credits. The default spending limit is 4,000 credits per user per month, which partners Sherweb and Universal.cloud put at about $40. Admins can change the limit. The rollout initially excludes several countries, including Australia, Germany, France and Spain.

Will my Copilot Business bill go up automatically? ▼

Only if people use the metered features. Grey Matter, a Microsoft partner, says you are charged only for credits actually consumed, up to the spending limit your admins set, and that setting the limit to zero disables usage-based billing. Partners disagree on whether existing licenses are affected when you add seats after December 1, so ask your Microsoft provider.

Does ChatGPT Business have usage-based charges too? ▼

Partly. OpenAI's help article says standard ChatGPT Business seats have a fixed per-user monthly price, Codex seats are usage-based and need workspace credits, and any workspace can purchase credits for usage beyond the included limits. The difference from Copilot's new default is that extra ChatGPT Business spending starts when someone buys credits.

Which is cheaper for a five-person team, Copilot Business or ChatGPT Business? ▼

On seat price alone they are close. Microsoft listed the Copilot Business add-on at $18 per user per month paid yearly (down from $21) on November 5, 2026, on top of a Microsoft 365 plan. ChatGPT Business lists at $20 annual or $25 monthly. Through an OpenAI SMB Channel Partner's referral link, OpenAI's partner promotion takes 20% off up to five Standard seats for 48 months, so five monthly seats cost $100 instead of $125. Copilot's default credit cap can add up to about $200 a month for five users if nobody lowers it.

How do I stop surprise Copilot Credit charges? ▼

In the Microsoft 365 admin center, go to Copilot, then Cost management. Set policy-level and user-level spending limits (zero if you do not want metered features), turn off Auto-apply new services, limit who can use Cowork, and set budget alerts that go to whoever pays the bills.

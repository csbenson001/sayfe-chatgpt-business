Most small business owners think of ChatGPT as something you talk to. You open it, ask a question, get an answer, close the tab. That mental model is now out of date. As of June 17, 2026, OpenAI relaunched Scheduled Tasks with its own dedicated Scheduled page in the sidebar — a feature that flips the relationship entirely. Instead of you going to ChatGPT, ChatGPT comes to you: at 6 a.m., every Monday, or the moment a number on a webpage changes.

This is the quiet feature that turns a chatbot into something closer to a tireless junior employee. It doesn't sleep, doesn't forget, and doesn't need a reminder to send the reminder. For a business owner wearing six hats, that's not a novelty — it's leverage. And here's the part that matters for anyone choosing a plan: the number of tasks you can run at once depends directly on which tier you're on, and ChatGPT Business gets double what the consumer Plus plan does.

This playbook covers exactly how Scheduled Tasks works, the limits you need to know before you rely on it, and ten concrete tasks you can set up this afternoon to put recurring busywork on autopilot.

The 30-second version: ChatGPT Scheduled Tasks lets you ask ChatGPT — in plain English — to do work on a schedule: send reminders, run recurring jobs like a daily briefing, or monitor the web and your connected apps and ping you only when something changes. You manage everything from a new Scheduled page in the sidebar. It's a paid-only feature, capped at roughly one run per hour, and your ceiling on active tasks scales with your plan: Go 3, Plus 5, Business & Edu 10, Pro & Enterprise 15.

What Scheduled Tasks Actually Does

Think of a scheduled task as a standing instruction with a clock attached. You describe what you want and when you want it, in natural language, and ChatGPT confirms the schedule and then runs on its own — whether or not your browser is open. There are three broad shapes this takes:

  • Reminders — one-off or recurring nudges. "Remind me to submit quarterly sales tax on the 15th of every month."
  • Recurring work — a job that produces something each time it runs. "Every weekday at 7 a.m., write me a 5-bullet briefing on overnight news in commercial real estate."
  • Monitoring — ChatGPT checks the web or a connected app on a cadence and notifies you only when there's something worth reporting. "Watch this competitor's pricing page and tell me if the price changes."

That third category is the sleeper. A monitoring task is the difference between "I should really keep an eye on that" and actually keeping an eye on it — without the check ever landing on your to-do list. It's the closest thing a solo operator has to an assistant whose entire job is to notice things.

How you set one up

There are two entry points. You can select a scheduled-tasks-capable model from the model picker and simply phrase your request with a time ("...every Monday at 8 a.m."), or you can open the Scheduled page in the sidebar and create the task there directly. Either way you describe the task and its timing conversationally, ChatGPT reads it back to confirm, and it's live. You can schedule for a precise time or a broad window — "morning," "afternoon," or "evening" — if the exact minute doesn't matter.

The Scheduled page is also your control panel. It shows every active task, when each one runs next, and lets you pause, resume, edit, or delete any of them in one place. No cron syntax, no configuration files — it's the same conversational interface you already use, pointed at the calendar.

The Limits You Need to Know First

Before you build your automation empire, read this section. Scheduled Tasks is powerful but deliberately fenced, and knowing the fences up front saves frustration.

⚠️ Five constraints to plan around: Scheduled Tasks (1) is paid-only — there's no free-tier access; (2) runs at most about once per hour, so it's not for second-by-second monitoring; (3) can't use voice, uploaded files, or Custom GPTs inside a task; (4) auto-pauses a task you keep ignoring, so notifications you never open will quietly stop; and (5) enforces a hard cap on active tasks per plan. Design around these and the feature is rock-solid; fight them and you'll be disappointed.

The active-task cap is the one that most directly affects your plan choice. Here's how the tiers stack up:

Plan Active Scheduled Tasks Best For
ChatGPT Go Up to 3 Personal reminders only
ChatGPT Plus Up to 5 A solo user, light automation
ChatGPT Business ✓ Up to 10 A small team running real workflows
ChatGPT Edu Up to 10 Schools and programs
ChatGPT Pro / Enterprise Up to 15 Heavy individual or org-wide use

The practical read for a small business: Plus gives one person five standing jobs, which fills up fast the moment you get serious. Business doubles that to ten and layers on the things a team actually needs — shared workspace administration, SSO, and the contractual guarantee that your data is never used to train models. 10 Scheduled Tasks to Put Your Business on Autopilot

Enough theory. Here are ten tasks you can set up today, grouped by what they buy you. Each one is a standing instruction you'd otherwise have to remember to do yourself.

Stay informed without doomscrolling

  1. The 7 a.m. industry briefing. "Every weekday at 7 a.m., give me a 5-bullet summary of the most important overnight news in [your industry], with one line on why each matters to my business." You start the day informed instead of catching up at lunch.
  2. Competitor price watch. "Check [competitor URL] once a day and notify me only if their pricing or plan structure changes." This is monitoring at its best — silence until it matters.
  3. Regulation and deadline radar. "Once a week, check for any new [state/industry] compliance or licensing deadlines and flag anything due in the next 30 days." A missed filing costs far more than a scheduled task.

Never drop the recurring ball

  1. Invoice and payment follow-ups. "Every Monday at 9 a.m., remind me which invoices are outstanding past 30 days so I can send follow-ups." Cash flow is a game of not forgetting.
  2. Tax and payroll reminders. "Remind me three business days before quarterly estimated taxes are due, every quarter." The kind of deadline that's expensive to miss and easy to forget.
  3. Weekly content calendar. "Every Friday afternoon, draft five social post ideas for next week tied to what's trending in my industry." Turns "I should post more" into a queue that refills itself — a natural fit for marketing teams and agencies.

Turn recurring reports into zero-effort output

  1. Monday metrics recap. "Every Monday at 8 a.m., write a short summary template for last week's key numbers so I just drop in the figures." Structure without the blank-page tax.
  2. Client check-in prompts. "Every two weeks, remind me which clients I haven't contacted recently and suggest a reason to reach out." Relationship maintenance on a timer — gold for consultants and professional services.
  3. End-of-month owner's digest. "On the last business day of each month, give me a checklist of everything I should review before closing the books." Your monthly close, pre-assembled.
  4. Lead-source review. "Every Wednesday, prompt me to log where this week's new leads came from and summarize the pattern." Small habit, compounding insight — especially for real estate and sales-driven businesses.
Pro tip: Since Business caps you at 10 active tasks, treat those slots like hires. Automate the recurring work that's genuinely costing you attention — the deadlines, the monitoring, the "I keep meaning to check that" items — and leave the one-off stuff to normal chats. Ten well-chosen standing jobs cover most of a small business's recurring cognitive load.

Scheduled Tasks vs. Workspace Agents: Which Do You Need?

It's easy to confuse two of ChatGPT's automation features, so let's draw the line clearly. Scheduled Tasks answer the question "when" — they run a job on a clock. Workspace Agents answer the question "how much work" — they carry out multi-step tasks autonomously, planning and calling tools across your connected apps. They're complementary: a scheduled task is often the trigger, and an agent is often the muscle.

The critical difference is cost behavior. Scheduled Tasks are included in your plan (within the active-task cap), so a daily briefing doesn't meter. Workspace Agents, by contrast, begin billing on credits July 6, 2026 — a run consumes roughly 5 to 25 credits. So the smart pattern is: use Scheduled Tasks freely for the "when," and be deliberate about when a scheduled job kicks off a credit-consuming agent run. Frequently Asked Questions

Is ChatGPT Scheduled Tasks free? ▼

No. Scheduled Tasks is a paid-only feature — it's not available on the free tier. It's included on ChatGPT Go, Plus, Business, Edu, Pro, and Enterprise, with the number of active tasks you can run varying by plan (Go 3, Plus 5, Business and Edu 10, Pro and Enterprise 15).

How many scheduled tasks can I have on ChatGPT Business? ▼

ChatGPT Business allows up to 10 active scheduled tasks per user — double the 5 available on the consumer Plus plan. Edu also allows 10, while Pro and Enterprise allow up to 15. If you consistently need more standing automations than your plan allows, that's a signal to move up a tier.

Can ChatGPT monitor a website and alert me when something changes? ▼

Yes. Monitoring is one of the three core uses of Scheduled Tasks. You can ask ChatGPT to check the web or a connected app on a schedule and notify you only when there's a meaningful change — for example, watching a competitor's pricing page. Note that tasks run at most about once per hour, so it's suited to daily or hourly monitoring rather than real-time alerts.

What are the main limitations of Scheduled Tasks? ▼

Scheduled Tasks runs at most roughly once per hour, cannot use voice, uploaded files, or Custom GPTs inside a task, and will automatically pause a task whose notifications you repeatedly ignore. It's also paid-only and capped at a set number of active tasks per plan. Within those boundaries it's reliable — the constraints mostly rule out real-time and file-based use cases.

How is Scheduled Tasks different from Workspace Agents? ▼

Scheduled Tasks control the timing — they run a job on a clock. Workspace Agents do the heavy lifting — autonomous, multi-step work across your connected apps. Scheduled Tasks are included in your plan within the active-task cap; Workspace Agent runs invoked inside ChatGPT begin billing on credits from July 6, 2026 (roughly 5–25 credits per run). Many businesses use a scheduled task as the trigger and an agent as the worker, and manage the credit cost accordingly.

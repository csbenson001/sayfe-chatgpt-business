Short answer: yes, bookkeepers can get real value from ChatGPT, and most already use something like it. The question is not whether to use it but where the line sits. Use a firm-controlled business workspace, never personal accounts. Keep client identifiers out: no Social Security numbers or EINs, no bank or card numbers, no W-9s, no full ledgers with names attached. Use ChatGPT for the work around the numbers, such as reconciliation notes, close checklists, client emails and Excel formulas. And write down your rules, because almost nobody has.

That last point is the one the data makes hard to ignore. In Financial Cents' 2026 State of AI in Accounting & Bookkeeping report (486 accounting and bookkeeping professionals surveyed July 15 to August 7, 2026), 95% said they are already using or exploring AI, and 96% of AI-active firms use a general assistant such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot. Yet "of the 350+ firms actively using AI, 87% have no formal written AI policy." With year-end close and 1099 season starting in the next eight weeks, this is the right time to fix that. Below: what to keep out, the workflows that are worth it, what your obligations look like, and what a firm workspace costs a two- to five-person practice.

What Bookkeepers Are Actually Doing With AI

The Financial Cents survey is useful because it is honest about results. A few findings worth knowing before you plan anything:

  • Use is near-universal, depth is not. Only 11% describe AI as embedded in daily workflows across much of the firm.
  • Returns are modest so far. "Only 1 in 5 firms can point to a measurable return on their AI investment," and "more than half of AI users save 3 hours a week or less." Only 15% save seven hours or more.
  • Trust is low, which is healthy. "Only 19% trust AI enough to use it with limited review." Accuracy was the top worry in free-text answers (38%), ahead of data security and confidentiality (19%).
  • Security is the main reason holdouts hold out. Among respondents not yet using AI, data security and client confidentiality was the biggest barrier, at 22%.

Financial Cents sells practice-management software, so read its marketing claims on the same page with that in mind. The survey numbers above are its research findings. Our read: the firms that get past three hours a week are the ones that pick a few repeatable jobs, build prompts and templates for them, and stop treating ChatGPT as a search box.

The One Rule: What Never Goes Into ChatGPT

A bookkeeper's files are full of the most sensitive data a small business has. OpenAI says it does not train on ChatGPT Business data by default (its help center puts it as "OpenAI won't train on your workspace's data"), and its business data page lists AES-256 encryption at rest and TLS 1.2 or higher in transit. Those are good protections. They are not a reason to paste a client's bank feed into a chat. Most bookkeeping tasks do not need identifiers at all, so the simplest policy is to leave them out every time.

Two habits make this easy. First, describe the pattern, not the client: "a landscaping company with seasonal payroll" is enough context for almost any drafting task. Second, when you need help with a spreadsheet, rebuild the structure with made-up rows rather than uploading the real file. You get the same formula without the risk. Month-End Close and 1099 Season: Workflows Worth Building

Month-end and year-end close

  • Close checklists by client type. Ask for a month-end checklist for a cash-basis service business, then a separate one for an inventory business, and edit them into your standard. Reuse them every month in a shared ChatGPT project.
  • Reconciliation reasoning. Describe the discrepancy in numbers only and ask for the most likely causes and the order to check them. It is a second pair of eyes, not an answer.
  • Commentary from finished numbers. Paste the totals you have already prepared (revenue, gross margin, top expense changes, no names) and ask for a three-paragraph plain-English summary the owner will actually read.
  • Spreadsheet work. Formulas for aging buckets, variance columns, lookups between exports and clean-up of messy date formats. See our guide to ChatGPT in Excel and Google Sheets.
  • Year-end adjusting entry explainers. Draft the short note that tells a client why you booked accrued expenses or reclassified a loan payment, in words they understand.

1099 season

The IRS instructions for Form 1099-NEC say: "Section 6071(c) requires you to file Form 1099-NEC on or before January 31." That makes December the month to chase missing vendor information. ChatGPT helps with the chasing, not the data:

  • W-9 request emails. A polite first request, a firmer second one, and a version your client can send to their own contractors. The W-9s themselves go into your secure portal, never into ChatGPT.
  • Client explainers. A one-page "who gets a 1099 and who doesn't" sheet in plain English, which you review against the IRS instructions before sending.
  • Vendor list triage, without names. Describe the categories ("12 contractors, 3 paid by card, 2 incorporated, 1 foreign") and ask for the questions you need to answer for each group.
  • Deadline calendars. A December-to-January work plan by client, built from dates you confirm against IRS sources.

Verify anything that sounds like a rule. ChatGPT explains well and drafts fast, but it can state an outdated threshold or exception with total confidence. Treat it like a capable new hire whose work you check.

What Your Obligations Look Like

Bookkeeping has no single regulator, so your duties come from your client contracts, your state, and, depending on your services, federal rules. Three points to check with your advisor:

  • The FTC Safeguards Rule. The FTC's business guide lists "tax preparation firms" among its examples of financial institutions covered by the rule. It does not name bookkeepers, so whether a bookkeeping-only practice is covered is a question for counsel. If you are covered, the rule says to "monitor your service providers," and that "the buck still stops with you." It also requires notifying the FTC "no later than 30 days after discovery" of a breach involving at least 500 consumers' unencrypted information. The FTC has exempted firms holding information on fewer than 5,000 consumers from certain provisions.
  • Your client agreements. Many engagement letters promise confidentiality in general terms. Add a sentence saying the firm uses AI tools under business terms that exclude training on firm data, that client identifiers are not entered, and that a person reviews all output.
  • A written policy. One page: the approved workspace, banned inputs (the right-hand column above), the review rule, and who to tell after a mistake. That alone puts you ahead of 87% of AI-using firms in the survey.
The practical line: one firm workspace, no client identifiers, a person reviews every output, and a one-page written policy. A ChatGPT Business workspace handles the vendor side (no training on your data by default, encryption, admin control over who has a seat). The other three are your decisions, and they take an afternoon.

Why a business workspace rather than personal accounts? Control. When a seasonal helper leaves in February, you remove the seat from the admin console, and the firm's prompts, templates and project history stay with the firm. With personal accounts, they leave with the person. Our ChatGPT Business vs. Plus comparison covers the rest of the difference, and the ChatGPT data privacy guide covers security in more depth.

Be clear about the limits too. OpenAI's help center says that if you need "Zero Data Retention, BAAs, or other sales-led options," you should "use a contracted offering instead of self-serve ChatGPT Business."

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it safe for bookkeepers to use ChatGPT with client data? ▼

Use a firm-controlled business workspace and keep client identifiers out. OpenAI says it does not train on ChatGPT Business data by default and encrypts data at rest and in transit. Even so, most bookkeeping tasks do not need names, account numbers, SSNs, EINs or W-9s, so the safest policy is to never enter them and to have a person review every output.

What can bookkeepers use ChatGPT for during month-end close? ▼

Close checklists by client type, reasoning through a reconciliation difference described in numbers only, plain-English commentary from totals you have already prepared, Excel and Google Sheets formulas built from sample rows, and short explainers for adjusting entries. Keep the real ledgers and bank data in your accounting system.

Can ChatGPT help with 1099 season? ▼

Yes, with the communication around it. The IRS instructions say Form 1099-NEC must be filed on or before January 31. ChatGPT can draft W-9 request emails, client explainers and a December work plan. The W-9s and taxpayer identification numbers themselves belong in your secure portal and filing software, not in ChatGPT, and any rule it states should be checked against the IRS instructions.

Do bookkeepers need a written AI policy? ▼

It is the cheapest protection you can add. Financial Cents' 2026 survey found that 87% of firms actively using AI have no formal written AI policy. One page covering the approved workspace, banned inputs, the review rule and what to do after a mistake is enough for most small firms. If your firm also prepares returns, check your FTC Safeguards Rule obligations with counsel.

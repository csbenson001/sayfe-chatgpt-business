ChatGPT Business Usage Limits Change in December: What the Shared ChatGPT + Codex Allowance Means for Your Team

Short answer: starting in December 2026, ChatGPT Business (along with Plus and Pro) will draw ChatGPT and Codex usage from one shared, token-based allowance instead of separate buckets. Chats with Instant models won't count against it. Heavier models and longer agent-style tasks will burn it faster. When someone runs out, they can wait for the reset, move to a bigger seat, or use extra credits. If your team mostly uses ChatGPT for quick everyday chat, you'll barely notice. If you have one or two people living in Codex or long multi-step tasks, you need a plan before December.

The change was reported on November 1, 2026 by The Decoder, citing an updated OpenAI support page on ChatGPT and Codex usage limits. It lands two days after OpenAI's Codex lead, Thibault Sottiaux, said limits on Business Premium seats were being doubled effective October 29 (Data Studios, Oct 30). Put together, the direction is clear: OpenAI is moving away from "all-you-can-prompt" and toward a metered allowance, with bigger seats for the people who need more.

Here's what that means in practice for a 5-to-50 person company, who on your team will actually feel it, and a four-step plan to get ahead of it.

The 30-second version: From December, one token budget covers both ChatGPT and Codex on Business, Plus and Pro. Instant-model chats are excluded. You can check your balance and reset time in Settings. At the limit you can wait, upgrade, or buy credits. On Business, the levers are Premium seats (OpenAI says 5x the usage of Standard, no five-hour limit, weekly resets) and shared workspace credits controlled by the workspace owner. OpenAI has not published the size of the allowance or an exact start date, so plan around behavior, not numbers.

What Exactly Is Changing in December

Until now, most people thought about ChatGPT and Codex as two separate meters. You had chat limits, and if you used Codex you had separate Codex limits (often expressed as a five-hour window plus a weekly cap). Per The Decoder's reading of OpenAI's updated help article, that split goes away in December for Plus, Pro and Business:

  • One budget, two products. ChatGPT and Codex usage come out of the same token-based allowance.
  • Cost depends on the work. "More complex models and tasks eat through the budget faster." A quick reply costs very little. A long reasoning task or a big Codex job costs a lot more.
  • Instant is free of the meter. Chats with Instant models don't count toward the budget. For a typical office team, that's the most important line in the whole change.
  • You can see it. Balance and reset times show up in Settings.
  • Three options at the limit: wait for the reset, upgrade, or buy extra credits.

The Decoder's article doesn't name Free, Go or Enterprise as part of the shared budget, and it doesn't give an allowance size. We couldn't load OpenAI's help article directly for this post, so treat any specific number you see elsewhere with suspicion until OpenAI publishes it. We'll update this post when it does.

Why it matters more than it sounds: a shared budget means one heavy activity can crowd out another. A teammate who spends the morning running a long Codex job may find their regular ChatGPT reasoning work slowed or capped that afternoon, even though they never "used up" chat in the old sense.

Who on Your Team Will Actually Feel It

Most small businesses don't have a uniform user base. They have a few power users and a lot of light ones. The shared budget hits those groups very differently.

The light user (most of your team). Drafting emails, summarizing a PDF, rewriting a proposal paragraph, answering a quick question. If most of this runs on Instant models, it doesn't touch the budget at all. For an office manager, a sales rep or a front-desk lead, December is probably a non-event.

The analyst or "deep work" user. The person who uploads a spreadsheet and asks for a full breakdown, runs long research questions, or chains multi-step tasks. These are the "more complex models and tasks" that burn through faster. This person may start seeing the limit for the first time.

The builder. Anyone using Codex: your one developer, the ops person who built automations, the marketer who generates landing pages. Their Codex work and their ChatGPT work now compete for the same pool. This is who is most likely to hit the wall mid-task.

If you run a consulting or marketing shop, you probably have more "deep work" and "builder" users per head than a typical company, so the change matters more to you. A law firm using ChatGPT mainly for drafting and summarizing will likely feel it less, unless someone is running long document-review sessions on reasoning models.

Your Levers on ChatGPT Business

This is where Business pulls ahead of a stack of individual Plus accounts. On Plus, each person is on their own; when they hit the limit, they wait or pay individually. On Business, the company has three tools.

1. Mix Standard and Premium seats

OpenAI introduced Premium seats for ChatGPT Business on August 10, 2026 and made them available on August 25. According to OpenAI, Premium seats give 5x more usage than Standard, have no five-hour usage limit, and come with predictable weekly resets. You can mix Standard and Premium seats in the same workspace. And per Data Studios' account of Sottiaux's October 29 post, Business Premium limits were doubled that day.

The practical move: keep most people on Standard and give Premium only to the builders and heavy analysts. Check OpenAI's current pricing page for seat costs before you decide; we haven't quoted prices here because we couldn't verify them on that page for this post.

2. Shared workspace credits

OpenAI's Premium-seats announcement also says that when users hit a limit, "businesses can add shared workspace credits, managed by your workspace owner." That's the overflow valve: instead of a team member being stuck until next week, the owner can cover a spike with credits and decide who gets to use them.

3. Steer routine work to Instant

Because Instant-model chats don't count, the cheapest thing you can do is teach people to use Instant for routine work and save heavier models for tasks that need them. A one-page internal guide ("Instant for drafts and quick questions, reasoning models for analysis, Codex for builds") will stretch the allowance further than any seat change.

Plus vs. Business, through this lens: the shared budget arrives on both. The difference is control. Business gives you a workspace owner who can mix seat types, manage shared credits, and see who needs what, on top of the data-privacy and admin features that made Business the right call for most teams anyway. A Four-Step Plan Before December

  1. Find your heavy users this week. Ask who uses Codex, who runs long analysis or research tasks, and who has hit a limit before. In most 10-to-25 person teams it's two to four people.
  2. Write the "which model when" rule. One paragraph in your team chat or wiki. Instant for everyday work, reasoning models for analysis, Codex for builds. This protects the shared budget for the work that needs it.
  3. Decide on Premium for the few, not the many. If a builder is already hitting limits, they're the candidate. Don't upgrade everyone on spec; watch December usage in Settings first.
  4. Set a credit policy. Decide in advance who can request extra credits and roughly how much you're willing to spend in a month. If you've already set spend guardrails for agents (see our workspace agents credit guide), extend the same rules here.

How This Compares to What Competitors Are Doing

OpenAI isn't alone in moving toward metered AI. Microsoft's October 2026 Partner Center announcements say that, beginning December 1, 2026, new Microsoft 365 Copilot Business purchases move to offers that don't require a qualifying base subscription, and that promotional pricing (25% off when bundled with Business Basic, 15% off standalone for 1–300 seats on annual terms) runs through December 31, 2026 for eligible customers (Microsoft Learn). That makes Copilot easier to buy on its own just as ChatGPT's limits get more structured, so expect Microsoft resellers to push hard in December.

Anthropic, meanwhile, is courting young companies: on October 6 it expanded Claude for Startups with a free year of Claude Team (up to five premium seats) plus $1,000 in API credits for companies founded in the last five years or funded in the last two (TechCrunch).

Our honest take: the shared budget is a real change, but it's not a reason to switch tools. Every major vendor is converging on "a base allowance plus paid overflow." What matters is whether the tool fits your workflow and whether you can control spend. ChatGPT Business still gives you both, as long as you set it up deliberately.

Frequently Asked Questions

When does the shared ChatGPT and Codex usage limit start? ▼

OpenAI's updated help article says the change starts in December 2026 for Plus, Pro and Business, as reported by The Decoder on November 1, 2026. OpenAI has not published an exact start date or the size of the allowance.

Do Instant model chats count toward the new ChatGPT Business limit? ▼

No. As reported, chats with Instant models do not count toward the shared budget. Heavier models, long reasoning tasks and Codex work draw it down, and more complex tasks use it faster.

What happens when someone on ChatGPT Business hits the limit? ▼

They can wait for the reset, move to a bigger seat, or use extra credits. On Business, the workspace owner can add shared workspace credits and can mix Standard and Premium seats, which OpenAI says give 5x the usage of Standard with no five-hour limit and weekly resets.

Does this change affect ChatGPT Enterprise? ▼

The reporting on the December change names Plus, Pro and Business. It does not name Free, Go or Enterprise. Enterprise workspaces have their own admin usage-limit controls, so check with OpenAI or your partner for Enterprise specifics.

Should my whole team upgrade to Premium seats before December? ▼

Usually not. Most small-business users do everyday work that Instant models handle, and that doesn't count against the budget. Give Premium to the two to four people who use Codex or run long analysis tasks, then review usage in Settings after December before upgrading anyone else.
